# The Atlas is a faceted classification with a law-first citation order: mathematics occupies the top two levels, mechanism the third

- Source: the user, 2026-10-16; ratified by questionnaire the same day. The user's words: it is critical that anyone who looks at, touches, edits or uses any part of the Atlas understands that mathematics is at the top two levels and that mechanism, where most complexity-science thinking starts, is not present until the third level down.
- Scope: the Atlas's classification, every view of it, every document and interface that presents it, and every record Morphogen derives from it.

The Atlas is a faceted classification, not a taxonomy, ontology or data model: each record carries one value per facet, cited in a fixed order. The citation order is law architecture (D0, DΔ, Dτ, S, N), then substrate (P, F, L, E, R, C, G, X), then mechanism family, then subtype; record type is an attribute. Law and substrate are closed, mutually exclusive vocabularies; their product is the complete top-level route space, 40 routes. A mechanism family is the formalism implementing a law on a substrate, so it is conditioned on the route above it; families are an open, curated vocabulary with an intake path. Subtypes are navigation groupings, not an axis. MECE is claimed at the record-address level only. No presentation of the Atlas may place mechanism above law or substrate, and no document may call it a taxonomy.
